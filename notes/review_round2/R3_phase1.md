## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): from an adjacent-field reader's standpoint, this asks whether the research design, data construction, statistical reporting, and replication materials would survive scrutiny by specialists in the method used. I do not score it; I note only where a methodological limit changes what practitioners or policymakers may legitimately take from the findings.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat): the claims must match current evidence in the field, prior studies must be described faithfully, and technical terms and reported results must be free of factual error. I do not score it; from a capital-markets perspective I may flag institutional facts that an adjacent reader would need to be correct.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate with the methodology seat also eligible): the central thesis must hang together, its evidence must carry its claims, and no reasoning fallacy may undercut the main argument. I do not score it.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high priority, my dimension): the framing, key definitions, and stated implications must be legible to readers outside the core subfield (for example market-microstructure, accounting, policy, or emerging-markets readers), and any claim that reaches into another discipline or into practice must be backed rather than asserted. This includes whether institutional context is explained, whether stakeholder implications are spelled out at a strength the design supports, and whether external-validity limits are acknowledged.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by the journal-fit seat): organisation, clarity, quality of exhibits, and conformity with the conventions of the target outlet. I do not score it.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the journal-fit seat): whether the paper suits the configured outlet and makes an original and significant contribution for that readership. I do not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether core constructs and the institutional setting (market structure, rating-provider practices, disclosure regimes, index or investor channels) are defined so adjacent-field readers can follow them; whether implications for named stakeholders (rating providers, exchanges, regulators, investors, issuers) are stated, are concrete, and are calibrated to what an observational design can support; whether heterogeneity across jurisdictions and limits to external validity are discussed; whether links to adjacent literatures are substantiated rather than gestured at.
what_triggers_block: Practical or policy implications that assert causal or welfare conclusions the design cannot support, or a central construct whose meaning for practitioners is undefined, such that an adjacent-field reader would draw materially wrong conclusions from the paper.
what_triggers_warn: Implications or institutional context that are thin, generic, or unevenly calibrated (for example stakeholders named without a concrete, design-consistent takeaway, or cross-market heterogeneity and external-validity limits under-discussed), while the core framing remains interpretable to adjacent readers.
