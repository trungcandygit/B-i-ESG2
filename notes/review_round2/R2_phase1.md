## Contract Paraphrase

D1 methodology_rigor (mandatory, scored by the methodology seat): the design, data handling, statistical reporting and reproducibility must reach the bar a referee in this field expects. From a domain-accuracy angle, this matters to me only where a measurement choice rests on a domain fact (for example, how a rating provider defines and dates its coverage), but I do not score it.

D2 domain_accuracy (mandatory, my dimension): every claim about the sustainable-finance and ESG-rating literature, and every factual statement about rating providers, rating coverage, investor demand and greenwashing, must agree with current evidence; prior work must be represented as its authors stated it; domain terms must be used precisely; and the paper's own results must be described in terms the field would accept as accurate. I own this score.

D3 argumentative_coherence (mandatory, scored by the devil's advocate and methodology seats): the central thesis must hold together and the evidence must carry the claims without fallacy. I note domain-level framing problems that feed coherence but do not score it.

D4 cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, scored by the perspective seat): framing, definitions and implications should be readable by adjacent fields, and interdisciplinary claims must be supported. I do not score it.

D5 writing_and_structure (normal priority, scored by the EIC seat): organisation, exposition, exhibits and venue conventions. I do not score it.

D6 venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, scored by the EIC seat): fit with the configured venue and an original, significant contribution for that readership. Literature positioning that I assess under D2 bears on it, but I do not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: accurate description of how ESG rating providers initiate, define and date coverage; correct representation of the core literatures on ESG ratings as information or certification, rating disagreement, rater selection, investor demand for ESG and greenwashing; precise use of terms such as coverage, initiation, rating, score and disclosure; verifiable citations; interpretations of results that the sustainable-finance literature would accept
what_triggers_block: a load-bearing factual error about the rating provider or its coverage process, or a material misrepresentation or omission of a core sustainable-finance literature that changes the interpretation of the main result, which revision can repair
what_triggers_warn: incomplete engagement with relevant sustainable-finance literatures, imprecise domain terminology, or interpretations of the result that go somewhat beyond what the domain evidence supports without changing the main conclusion
what_triggers_fatal: the central construct or main claim rests on a domain fact that is false or on fabricated or unverifiable citations, so the core finding cannot be interpreted as stated
