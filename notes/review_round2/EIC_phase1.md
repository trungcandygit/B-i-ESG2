## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat). From an editorial-oversight view, this dimension asks whether the design, data handling, statistical reporting and replication affordances clear the bar that a referee in this field would apply. It is outside my scoring remit; I note only whether methodological claims are presented in a way the journal's readers can follow.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat). This dimension asks whether the claims match current evidence in the field, whether prior work is represented faithfully, and whether domain terms and facts are used correctly. It is not mine to score; editorially I care that the literature a sustainability-finance reader expects is visibly engaged.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate with the methodology seat eligible). This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together, whether evidence carries the claims, and whether any fallacy undermines the core argument. I do not score it; I watch only for title/abstract/conclusion promises that the body does not deliver.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by the perspective seat). This dimension asks whether framing, definitions and implications are legible to readers from adjacent fields and whether interdisciplinary claims are substantiated. I do not score it.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by me). This dimension asks whether the manuscript is organised so that the question, design, results and implications are easy to locate; whether exposition is clear; whether exhibits are well built and referenced; and whether the paper follows the conventions of a sustainability-focused finance journal (section structure, keywords, classification codes, length, exhibit limits, disclosure statements).

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by me). This dimension asks whether the paper belongs in a sustainability-focused finance journal and whether it makes an original, significant contribution for that readership. The reference case is a prior desk rejection for appropriateness, so I must judge whether the question is a sustainability question (about how ESG information, ratings, or sustainability-motivated capital affect firms and markets) rather than a generic corporate-finance or econometric-method question that happens to use an ESG variable, and whether the title, abstract, introduction, contribution statement, keywords and implications signal that.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: A conventional and consistent section sequence with a roadmap that matches the headings; a front matter (title, abstract, keywords, classification codes) whose vocabulary matches the body; exhibits that are cited before they appear, carry descriptive captions and short notes, and are within venue limits; prose that states findings plainly without hedging stacks or overclaiming; length appropriate to a journal article.
what_triggers_block: The manuscript cannot be followed as a journal article: missing or misordered core sections, exhibits unreadable or unreferenced, or front matter that contradicts the body, such that a handling editor could not send it to referees without restructuring.
what_triggers_warn: The manuscript is reviewable but has presentation problems that blunt its message for the target readership, such as a title or abstract dominated by method jargon, keywords that omit the sustainability vocabulary of the venue, roadmap or cross-reference mismatches, overlong notes, or results that live mainly in an appendix.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: Whether the research question is posed as a sustainability-finance question; whether the stated contribution is to the literature on ESG ratings, sustainability information and investor behaviour rather than only to methods or general valuation; whether title, abstract, opening paragraph, keywords and implications use the venue's vocabulary and speak to its readers (investors, rating providers, regulators, sustainability-minded managers); whether the finding is new relative to prior work and matters for sustainable-finance practice or policy.
what_triggers_block: The paper's question and contribution are framed primarily as a corporate-finance, valuation or econometric-design exercise in which the sustainability content is incidental, so that a sustainability-finance editor would reasonably judge it out of scope, yet the substance could be reframed for that readership without new data.
what_triggers_warn: The paper is in scope and its sustainability content is real, but the framing under-sells it: the sustainability mechanism, stakeholder relevance or practical implications are thin, buried, or stated in generic terms, or the contribution relative to the closest ESG-rating literature is not sharply stated.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript has no sustainability-finance question at all, or its contribution duplicates existing published work or a companion study without a distinct question, so that no reframing achievable without new data or new analysis could make it an original contribution for a sustainability-focused finance readership.
