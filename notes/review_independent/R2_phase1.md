## Contract Paraphrase

D1 methodology_rigor (mandatory; scored by the methodology seat only). From a domain-accuracy standpoint, this dimension asks whether the design, data handling, statistical reporting and replication materials would satisfy a finance referee. I will not score it, but where a methodological choice rests on a factual claim about how ESG rating data are produced, stored, backfilled or revised by the provider, I will report the factual part under D2 and leave the econometric consequence to the methodology seat.

D2 domain_accuracy (mandatory; my dimension). Claims must be consistent with the current sustainable-finance evidence base, every cited work must be represented as it actually says (authors, year, venue, finding), and domain facts must be right: what the rating provider covers, when and how its scores are produced and revised, how coverage expands, what the rating-disagreement and ESG-valuation literatures have found. Terms such as "coverage initiation", "first ESG score", "rated firm" and "valuation" must be used in a way the field would recognise, and the construct must measure what the paper says it measures.

D3 argumentative_coherence (mandatory; scored by the devil's advocate and methodology seats). The central thesis must hang together internally: the evidence presented must support the claims made, and no fallacy (for example reverse causation read as causation, or a null read as proof of absence) may undermine the main argument. I do not score it; domain-level misreadings that feed an incoherence are reported under D2.

D4 cross_disciplinary_relevance (high; scored by the perspective seat). Framing, definitions and implications should be accessible to readers from adjacent fields, and any interdisciplinary claim must be substantiated. Not scored by me.

D5 writing_and_structure (normal; scored by the EIC seat). Organisation, clarity, exhibit quality and compliance with venue conventions. Not scored by me.

D6 venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory; scored by the EIC seat). The manuscript must fit the configured venue and make an original, significant contribution for its readers. Not scored by me, although my view of how the contribution sits against prior work is input the EIC may use.

Failure conditions. F1: any fatal block on a mandatory dimension leads to reject. F2: any block on a mandatory dimension leads to major revision. F3: if a majority of reviewers find two or more mandatory dimensions at warn or worse, major revision. F4: any block on the high-priority dimension leads to major revision. F5: any warn or worse on any dimension leads to minor revision. F0: every dimension pass across all reviewers leads to accept. The more severe condition prevails; only the synthesizer applies these.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether every cited work exists with correct bibliographic data and is represented faithfully; whether statements about the ESG rating provider (coverage universe, expansion history, scoring methodology, backfilling and restatement of scores, data vintages) are factually correct and sourced; whether the literature on ESG ratings and firm value, rating disagreement, investor demand for rated firms, and rating coverage determinants is covered and positioned accurately; whether the coverage-initiation construct validly captures the event it is claimed to capture.
what_triggers_block: One or more material domain errors that the core claim depends on but that are correctable in revision, such as a misrepresented key prior finding, an incorrect description of how the provider assigns or backdates first scores, a nonexistent or materially mis-cited reference used to support a claim, or a coverage-initiation construct whose validity is not established and whose failure would change the interpretation of the main result.
what_triggers_warn: Domain issues that weaken positioning or precision without undermining the core claim, such as omission of important related literature, minor bibliographic errors, imprecise terminology, or provider facts asserted without a source.
what_triggers_fatal: The core claim rests on a domain premise that is demonstrably false and cannot be repaired within the paper's data, such as a first-score date that by provider design does not record when coverage began, or evidence that the central finding merely restates an established published result that the paper misrepresents as new.
