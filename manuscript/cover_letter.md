The paper asks whether the first ESG score that a rating provider assigns to a listed firm changes how the equity market values that firm. Using the staggered first appearance of LSEG ESG scores for 675 non-financial firms in Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, and Thailand, and heterogeneity-robust difference-in-differences estimators, we find no change in market-to-book ratios after the first score, while rated firms had gained value faster than comparable firms in the years before it. A static two-way fixed effects regression would instead attribute a valuation gain to coverage. We registered the hypotheses, sample rules, estimator, and robustness checks in a pre-analysis plan before estimation and report all pre-specified results, including a placebo test that fails.

Companion study disclosure. The data set used here also underlies a companion study by the same authors: "GRI Adoption and Corporate Brownwashing: Board Governance Evidence from ASEAN-5," currently under review (minor revision, second round) at the International Journal of Management and Sustainability. The two papers do not overlap in research question, analysis, or results. The companion study examines whether adoption of Global Reporting Initiative standards and board characteristics relate to the gap between ESG scores and controversy scores, using 1,506 firm-years of ESG-rated firms and two-way fixed effects models. The present paper studies the market valuation consequences of receiving a first ESG score, uses the full listed universe including never-rated firms, and applies a staggered difference-in-differences design. No variable used as an outcome or treatment, no estimate, and no table or figure appears in both papers, and the text was written independently; a word 8-gram comparison with the companion manuscript finds no shared 8-word sequence.
